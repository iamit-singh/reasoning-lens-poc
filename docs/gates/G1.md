## G1 is closed at 10 of 10

The last box was check 7, and it stayed open for the most ordinary reason in the project:
the recorder existed and had been verified on **one** item, and the full recording was
queued behind a measurement that wanted the same tier. It closed the moment the warm-cache
run went through — 49 analysis cassettes fell out of it as a by-product, because the
pipeline passes a cassette name on **every** call rather than only in mock mode, so the
recorded call and the production call are the same call on the same code path.

**What this unblocks is the whole reason G1 exists.** The frontend has been building
against fixtures with zero Lead dependency since W5; from here CI's integration-mock job
runs the entire bank with no LLM calls, and local development is free. Three separate
claims elsewhere in the plan assumed this already existed.

## Deviations, stated rather than smoothed over

### Check 3 — the taxonomy does not populate, so "harvested" was not available

§6.3 specifies `report_nominal.json` as *"harvested from a real W4 run, hand-checked"*, and
that is not achievable on this corpus. Over a full bank × 3 arms pass the classifier
produced:

| `linear` | `verification` | `subgoal_setting` | `backward_chaining` | `backtracking` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 252 | 35 | 22 | 1 | **0** |

**No trace contains all five classes.** The options were to hand-author a file called
"nominal" and let a reader assume it came from a run, or to commit both and label them.
Both are committed: `report_nominal.json` is authored and carries the five classes;
`report_measured.json` is real and carries realistic text lengths and null patterns. Tests
assert that authored fixtures use `fx-` ids and a `fixture000000000` pin and that the
measured one uses neither, so the two can never be confused.

This is the **third** time a phenomenon the plan assumed has failed to appear on this
model, after ADR-005 (traps do not reproduce) and ADR-006 (arms 1 and 2 do not separate).
It has consequences for M2's κ that are larger than its consequences for G1, and it gets
its own ADR.

### Check 8 — the freeze held, and a defect underneath it did not

`segmenter-frozen-v1` is intact: `segment.py` is unchanged and the twelve goldens are
byte-identical. But `step_id` — which C3.2 calls *"the join key for every label ever
written"* — **was not unique across the corpus**. `direct:direct-llm-0:0` named the first
step of all fourteen items, and 310 steps collapsed to 155 distinct ids.

The cause was upstream of the segmenter: the runner emitted a span id per *strategy* rather
than per *run*. `NormalizedTrace` enforces uniqueness within a trace, which is exactly what
hid it. The fix put the item id into the span id and kept it deterministic; **ordinals did
not move and no text changed step**, so the freeze's meaning is unaffected — only the id's
middle component differs, and no label existed to be detached.

**Found by check 9's own draw**, on its first run. That is the plan's ordering rule paying
for itself: had it surfaced in W6 it would have surfaced as a κ that made no sense, against
40 human labels nobody could repair.

## What a reader should take from this gate

The contract is stable. The three things most likely to move it later are all in the schema
already and all nullable: the escalation tier (M2-5), the consistency checker (M2-8), and
`measurement_context` (M2-17). After G1, a change to any of them that is **not** additive
requires a `schema_version` bump, regenerated fixtures and a note in the same PR — and
`additionalProperties: false` plus the `const` on `schema_version` are what make that a
build failure rather than a promise.
